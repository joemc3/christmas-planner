# Principles

## 1. Remaining is the number

Of budget, spent and remaining, remaining is what the user acts on. It is the largest or the first number everywhere the three appear. Over budget is a visible state, not a red decimal: the number, the bar and the row all say so.

## 2. The date is the hero of the home screen

Days to Christmas is context. The Start Ordering By date is the point. Before it, show the date and the days left. On or after it, the home screen leads with how many gifts still need ordering and takes the user straight to them.

## 3. Three states, one tap

A gift is To Buy, Ordered or Purchased; a menu item is To Buy or Purchased. Moving forward is one tap from the list, without opening the item. The states read as a sequence, not as a dropdown.

## 4. One person per screen

The gifts area is a list of people, then one person's gifts. A gift always belongs to someone; there is no cross-person gift list in this version. The person's three numbers sit above their gifts and update as statuses change.

## 5. Lists, not dashboards

The home screen is a summary and a door to two places. Everything else is a list with a header. No charts beyond a single bar for budget used. No cards inside cards.

## 6. Empty states teach

A plan with no recipients, a recipient with no gifts, a meal with no items: each empty state says what to add and offers the one action, in the place the first row will appear.

## 7. Phone first, laptop honest

Designed at phone width, where one hand and a thumb is the input. At laptop width the same screens widen; the list of people and the selected person may sit side by side, but nothing is added that the phone does not have.

## 8. Both themes, equal care

Light and dark are both first-class. Every colour is a role with a value in each theme. Dark is not inverted light; it is designed.
