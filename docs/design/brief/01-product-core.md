# What Christmas Planner is

One place to plan a family Christmas and stay inside the money. You list the people you are buying for and what each may cost, list the gifts under each person, and the app tells you what you have spent, what is left, and the date by which ordering has to start so that everything is in the house by 10 December. The same for the two Christmas meals: who is coming, what it may cost, what is on the menu, what is bought.

It replaces a spreadsheet and a notes app. It is not a calendar, a to-do app or a wish-list service.

## The four things it must do

1. **Show the money at a glance.** For gifts and for meals: budget, spent, remaining. For each person and each meal the same three numbers. Never make the user add anything up.
2. **Hold the gifts per person.** Name, price, where it comes from, and one of three states: To Buy, Ordered, Purchased.
3. **Tell the user when to start ordering.** One date, derived from 10 December minus a buffer minus their shipping time. Before the date it is a countdown; after it, every gift still To Buy is a problem the app points at.
4. **Hold the two meals.** Christmas Eve and Christmas Day, each with a guest count, a budget and a menu whose items are To Buy or Purchased.

## Who uses it

One person in a household who owns Christmas. Phone in hand in a shop or on the sofa; laptop at the kitchen table on a Sunday. Starts in October, uses it heavily for eight weeks, opens it once the following year to see what they did.

They are not a power user. They have a spreadsheet they hate and a notes app full of half-ideas. The app wins if it is faster than the spreadsheet for "how much is left for Mum" and calmer than the notes app for "what have I not ordered yet".

## The moments that matter

- **First open, October.** Empty. The app has to make the first recipient and the first gift feel like a two-minute job, and show the ordering date immediately so the user knows the clock is running.
- **In a shop.** Thumb on a phone, one hand. "Is this inside Mum's budget?" then mark it Purchased.
- **Late November.** The ordering date arrives. The app must make the gifts that still say To Buy impossible to miss, without nagging about the ones already handled.
- **Mid December.** Everything ordered. The meals take over: who is coming, what still needs buying.
- **Next October.** Open last year, see what was spent, start this year's plan.

## Tone

Warm but not cute. This is a planning tool that happens to be about Christmas, not a Christmas-themed toy. No snowflakes, no countdown confetti, no "ho ho ho". The festive feeling comes from the content (the names, the gifts, the menu), and from a palette that feels like December without shouting it.
